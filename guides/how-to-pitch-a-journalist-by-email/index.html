How to pitch a journalist by email: a practical guide

Draft by AI · Reviewed by Rizky Wicaksono · 25 November 2026 · 5 min read

In short

A good pitch is short, personal and relevant. It explains the story in two or three sentences, says why it fits this journalist’s beat, and makes it easy to say yes. Follow up once, politely, accept a no, and never offer inducements.

Do your research first

Read what the journalist has written recently and note their beat, their angle and their audience. A pitch that fits a journalist’s recent work is far more likely to get a reply than a generic one. If you cannot explain why this person is the right reader, do not send it.

Choose outlets carefully too. The Dewan Pers records press companies that choose to be verified. Verification is voluntary and is not the same as registration, so an outlet that is not on the list is not automatically illegitimate, but the list is a useful signal when you build a media list.

Write a subject line that says the story

Make the subject line a short description of the story, not a teaser. “Local bakery chain opens its tenth branch in South Jakarta” works better than “Exciting news from our client”. Skip all caps and exclamation marks.

Use the first two sentences well

Lead with the story and why it matters to this journalist’s readers. Add one line that shows you know their work, such as a recent article it connects to. Keep the whole email short, ideally under about 200 words (Muck Rack’s State of Journalism research reports that 69% of journalists prefer pitches under 200 words), and write in the language the journalist works in.

Say what you can offer

  • A spokesperson who is available, and when.
  • Data or a result with a source.
  • Photos, video or documents ready to use.
  • An exclusive or an early look, if you can truly offer one.

Send it one by one

Send each pitch separately and avoid mass emails with everyone copied. Personalise at least one line. Pitching a story to many journalists at once can be fine, but each should feel written for them.

Follow up once, then let go

If you hear nothing, one short follow-up after a few days is reasonable. Add something new, such as a new fact or a deadline, rather than repeating the first email. If the answer is no, thank them and keep the relationship for the next story.

Some journalists also use WhatsApp for time-sensitive news, but only message them there if they have said they are comfortable with it. Never send mass messages.

Keep it clean

Journalists must follow the Journalistic Code of Ethics (Press Law, Article 7 paragraph 2), and Article 6 of the code is the rule against abusing the profession or accepting bribes. A pitch should win on relevance, never on inducements. If you invite journalists to an event, keep hospitality modest and transparent.

Track what works

Note which subject lines, angles and send times get replies. Over a few campaigns, patterns appear, and your pitches improve with them.

Frequently asked questions

How long should a pitch email be?

Under about 200 words. If a journalist wants more, they will ask.

How many times should I follow up?

Once, a few days after the first email, with something new. More than that tends to hurt the relationship.

Can I pitch on WhatsApp?

Only if the journalist is comfortable with it. Some use it for time-sensitive news, but never send mass messages.
