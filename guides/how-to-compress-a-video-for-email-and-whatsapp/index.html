How to compress a video for email and WhatsApp so it arrives in time

Draft by AI · Reviewed by Rizky Wicaksono · 25 November 2026 · 7 min read

In short

For email, a video has to fit the same 25 MB attachment limit as any file, so aim for about 18 MB. For WhatsApp, the help center lists a default video limit of 100 MB at 720p on WhatsApp Web (64 MB at 480p on slower connections). Video size is roughly bitrate times length, so the two levers that matter are resolution and length: 720p is plenty for a phone screen, and a one-minute clip fits in 18 MB at about 2.4 Mbps.

How big can a video be on email and WhatsApp?

  • Email: 25 MB for Gmail and Outlook.com attachments. Gmail turns anything bigger into a Google Drive link. Because attachments grow by about a third when they are encoded, aim for about 18 MB (the PDF guide explains why).
  • WhatsApp (as a video): on WhatsApp Web, the help center lists a default video limit of 100 MB and 720p for faster connections, and 64 MB and 480p for slower ones. Supported formats include MP4, MOV, MKV, AVI, FLV and 3GP.
  • WhatsApp (as a document): files sent with the Document option can be up to 2 GB. That is useful for a large file, but it is slow to download on mobile data.

How big will my video be?

A video’s size is its bitrate (how much data it uses each second) multiplied by its length. So for a fixed target size, a longer clip gets less data per second. Here is the total bitrate, picture plus sound, that fits in 18 MB:

  • 30 seconds: about 4.8 Mbps
  • 1 minute: about 2.4 Mbps
  • 2 minutes: about 1.2 Mbps
  • 3 minutes: about 0.8 Mbps

The less data per second, the blockier the picture gets, especially at high resolutions. For long clips, lower the resolution to 720p or 480p, or trim the video first. The MP4 Compressor warns you when your settings will look blocky.

Every cut is an editorial choice

When you trim a spokesperson’s statement into a 40-second clip for WhatsApp, you are doing what an editor does: deciding what the audience hears. That is normal PR work, but it carries a responsibility. A clip that changes the meaning of what was said, or leaves out the part a journalist would ask about, can cost you more trust than the coverage is worth.

A simple habit helps: send the short clip for speed, and offer the full, uncut recording alongside it. It tells the newsroom you have nothing to hide, and it lets them check context themselves. Name files clearly too, with the speaker, the event and the date, so a producer who finds the file a week later still knows what it is.

Speed matters as much as polish. A 720p preview that lands while the editor is still planning the day is often worth more than a perfect 4K file that arrives after the rundown is set. (Practitioner advice, not a newsroom rule.)

How PR teams use this

  • A spokesperson clip for an online newsroom. Send a 720p preview by WhatsApp or email so the editor can decide quickly.
  • Event highlights for media and partners. A short 720p cut under 18 MB fits in the same email as the press release.
  • B-roll for TV. Broadcasters usually want the best quality you have, so send a small preview and a link to the full-quality file, not a compressed copy only.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best size to email a video?

About 18 MB or less. Gmail and Outlook.com allow 25 MB, but encoding adds about a third, and some company mail servers allow less.

How do I send a video longer than WhatsApp allows?

Lower the resolution or trim it, or send it with the Document option, which WhatsApp allows up to 2 GB. For very large files, a cloud link is easier for the recipient.

Which resolution should I choose?

720p for most email and WhatsApp use. 1080p only if the clip is short or the target is large; 480p for long clips.

Can I turn a MOV from an iPhone into an MP4?

Yes. The tool reads MOV, WebM and MKV files and always saves the result as MP4.

Why is it slow?

Your own computer does the encoding, not a server. Longer and higher-resolution videos take more time, and the tab must stay open and visible.
