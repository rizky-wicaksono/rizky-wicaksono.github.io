PR for AI search: how to get your brand mentioned in AI answers

Guide & reviewed by Rizky Wicaksono · Drafted with AI assistance · 25 November 2026 · 6 min read

In short

Google says optimizing for its generative AI features is still SEO: be indexed, be eligible for a snippet, and publish helpful, people-first content. No special markup or AI files are needed. PR adds what content alone cannot: credible mentions elsewhere and consistent facts. No one outside the platforms knows the exact rules of every AI tool, so treat the rest as good practice, not a guarantee.

What GEO means, and what Google says

Generative engine optimization (GEO) is a term for making your brand and content easier for AI tools to find, understand and cite. It is a young field and the rules change. Google’s own guide on generative AI features says you do not need new machine-readable files, AI text files, markup or special formats to appear in its AI features, and that optimizing for them is still SEO. It also says Google Search ignores llms.txt files.

Be indexable and eligible for a snippet

According to Google, to be eligible for its generative AI features a page must be indexed and eligible to appear in Search with a snippet. Indexing is not guaranteed. In practice: let search engines crawl your pages, give each page a clear title and description, list your pages in a sitemap, and use Search Console to see what is indexed.

Publish helpful, people-first content

Google’s guidance on helpful content asks whether your content gives original information or analysis, goes into real depth, shows first-hand experience, cites its sources and is clear about who wrote it and why. It also asks readers to be told how the content was made, including when AI helped. Trust is the most important quality it describes.

Avoid scaled content

Google warns that producing content at scale mainly to manipulate rankings or AI responses violates its scaled content abuse spam policy, whether or not AI is used. For a person or a small brand, the lesson is simple: fewer, better pages beat many thin ones.

Earn credible mentions elsewhere (where PR helps)

This part is a practitioner’s view, not an official rule. AI answers tend to reflect what credible, independent sources say about a topic, so coverage in respected outlets, industry media and trusted directories supports both your reputation and your visibility. This is classic PR work: relevant stories, good relationships with journalists, and clear facts they can quote.

Keep your facts consistent

Use the same name spelling, job title, description and key facts across your website, LinkedIn, press releases and boilerplates. When sources agree, it is easier for both readers and machines to be sure who you are and what you do. Contradictions create confusion and wrong answers.

Check what AI tools say about you

Every month or two, ask a few AI tools about your brand or your name and note what they say and which sources they cite. If something is wrong, find the source it came from and fix or update that page. Over time, this shows you which sources matter.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need schema markup or an llms.txt file?

Google says no special markup or AI files are needed for its AI features, and that structured data is not required for generative AI search. Structured data can still help with rich results in regular search. Google Search ignores llms.txt.

Does GEO replace SEO?

For Google’s AI features, Google describes the work as still SEO. Clear, credible, people-first content helps in both.

Can I pay to be mentioned in AI answers?

There is no reliable way to buy it. Be wary of anyone who sells guaranteed AI mentions.

How long does it take to see results?

It depends on how fast new coverage and pages are indexed and picked up. Think in months, not days.
