<h2>Start with the objective</h2>
<p>Measurement starts before the campaign. Decide what the PR is meant to change: awareness, reputation, sign-ups, attendance, sales leads. Without an objective, every number looks impressive and none of them means anything.</p>

<h2>Separate outputs from outcomes</h2>
<p>Outputs are what you produce: press releases, interviews, articles published. Outcomes are what changes because of them: people visiting, registering, buying or changing their opinion. Report both, and put more weight on outcomes.</p>

<h2>Metrics worth tracking</h2>
<ul><li><strong>Coverage volume and quality:</strong> how many articles, in which outlets, and how relevant those outlets are to your audience. Grouping outlets into tiers, for example tier 1, 2 and 3, can make this clear.</li><li><strong>Message pull-through:</strong> how often your key messages appear in the coverage.</li><li><strong>Share of voice:</strong> your visibility compared with competitors on the same topic.</li><li><strong>Referral traffic and actions:</strong> visits from articles, plus sign-ups, enquiries or sales that follow.</li><li><strong>Sentiment:</strong> whether the tone toward you is positive, neutral or negative.</li><li><strong>Spokesperson visibility:</strong> interviews and quotes secured.</li></ul>

<h2>Using AMEC’s framework</h2>
<p>AMEC, the International Association for the Measurement and Evaluation of Communication, maintains the Barcelona Principles (now version 4.0), the most widely used reference for PR measurement. In everyday practice it comes down to a few habits: set a measurable goal before the campaign, look at outcomes as well as outputs, combine numbers with a read on quality, and say how you measured.</p><p>On AVE (advertising value equivalency), the cost of the space or airtime coverage would occupy if bought as advertising, AMEC does not treat it as the value of communication. The industry is still discussing it, and AVE remains common in Indonesian PR reporting, where many clients ask for it. Where it is used, it helps to present it as an estimate of media space value, state the rate card behind it, and report it next to the metrics above. AMEC’s “22 Reasons Not to Use AVEs” guide, available in Indonesian, sets out the reasoning behind its position.</p>

<h2>An example of pairing coverage with a result</h2>
<p>A coverage count is more convincing next to a business outcome. In the JAFF Market campaign, the 663 media coverages came alongside 1,120 approved business meetings on the platform. Together they tell a story: attention was generated, and it turned into participation.</p>

<h2>Report it simply</h2>
<p>Keep the monthly report short: the objective, the three or four most important numbers, the best pieces of coverage, what you learned and what you will change next month. Decision-makers read two clear pages; they skip twenty.</p>
